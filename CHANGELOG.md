# Changelog

## 1.1.1

- **Back to your search results, without searching again.** Opening a title from the results and coming back started the whole search over (every extension asked again, the list rebuilt). The results are now kept while the page is in your history, and the list is where you left it.
- **Cloudflare checks pass in seconds instead of timing out.** The hidden page passed a site's check after about 6 seconds, but the app did not notice: it waited the full 25 seconds, gave up and left the site alone for 10 minutes. It now sees the pass as it happens and tries it on the real request before believing it. Measured on two protected sites: 26 s before, 6 to 9 s now, and no more "left alone" after a check that was in fact passed.
- **No more empty window after a video opened at start.** A video opened while the app was still starting (a link or a file the app was started with) and then closed left the window empty until the app was restarted. The cause: when the start-up screen went away, every page was thrown away and built a second time, at every start. The app is now built once.
- **A playing video no longer downloads every subtitle file.** To put the working subtitles first, the app fetched all of them behind the video, in every language: 429 files in two minutes for one film, with sites answering "Too Many Requests". Now only the files of your subtitle language are looked at when the video starts; the others when you open the Subtitles menu.
- **Clone site works with the sandbox on.** Settings > General > Clone site (an extension's site under another address, for a site that moved or is blocked) saved the copy but it never appeared when the extension ran in the sandbox, which is the default. The copy is now made inside the sandbox and comes back at every start.
- **One more Android piece for extensions:** the ready-made link and address patterns (`android.util.Patterns`); an extension that uses them failed to load.
- **Less memory.** Unused memory could pile up to about 2.7 GB before it was cleaned (measured 70 seconds after a video that failed to load, with under 100 MB really in use). It is now cleaned much earlier: 605 MB at most in the same test.
- **The Home banner changes slides every 15 seconds** (was 9). Each change redraws the whole window for about a second, which was most of what an open, idle Home cost. Measured: about 10 % of a processor core before, 7.7 % now.
- **Less battery use with Home open in the background.** The Home banner's progress line redrew the whole window 8 times a second, even with another app in front or the window minimised (9-10 % of a processor core for nothing). It now pauses while the window isn't active and carries on where it stopped when you come back: 1.6 % in the background, 4 % minimised.
- **Settings read well in a half-screen window.** A drop-down kept its full width beside its description, which became a column of one or two words per line. Now a control that doesn't fit beside the text goes under it; switches stay beside it.
- **Light mode fixes.** On Home, the search and reload buttons and the extension picker were dark grey on the dark banner and could hardly be seen; over a banner they are now drawn light. On a title page, "Completed" / "Ongoing" and the next-episode line were dark blue on the picture; they now use the light shade of the accent. White extension logos with a see-through background (AniVortex) no longer vanish on the light tile.
- **Player menu:** *Audio delay* shows "Off" like the rows around it (it showed nothing).
- **A safer start with an empty data-folder setting.** An empty `-Dcloudstream.data=` made the app use the folder it was started from as its data folder, and *Reset app* would have deleted in that folder. An empty setting is now ignored.
- **No more "★ 0".** A title nobody has rated yet showed a zero score (the Home banner, poster badges, Explore, episode lists), which read as a terrible film. A missing score is now simply not shown.
- **Drop-down arrows sit at the right edge.** In every drop-down (Settings, Library, the title page) the arrow followed the text, so a wide box read "Dark ⌄" and then empty space.
- **The Extensions page counts the same way twice.** The header said "29 installed" while the list under it said "26 extensions": the header counted an extension once for every repository that lists it. Both now count what is installed. When your language or type filter hides some, the list says so ("24 of 26 shown").
- **Stats polish.** The activity grid fills its card in a wide window (it stopped at about four fifths). "All time" is the same size as the other three numbers. "1 day watched" and "1 title in the diary" instead of "1 days" and "1 titles".
- **Live DASH channels no longer drop pieces of sound.** The app refuses a live piece the player asks for twice, to stop a 2-second loop at the live edge (JIO TV). It also refused such repeats far behind the edge, where the player was re-reading a piece on purpose. On WILLOW, 30 s behind live, that was 26 refusals in 4 minutes, and each one skipped a piece of sound. Repeats are now refused only at the live edge.
- **No false "hang" report after the PC sleeps.** The freeze watcher counted the time the PC was asleep as a frozen window: a 52-minute "hang" report and freeze-log entry after one sleep. Time asleep is now left out.
- **Videos use about half the processor.** The standard player converts every frame on the processor, and the video library split that work over one thread per processor thread (16 on a test PC). Waking and syncing all of them cost more than the work itself. It now uses 2 threads (4 for very large windows). Same test video, same window: 0.85 of a core before, 0.46 now; still 24 fps, no dropped frames, same picture. Less heat and battery use while watching. The frame-timing wait also sleeps instead of spinning for its last 1.5 ms. On a weak or hot laptop, *Native GPU player (experimental)* in Settings > Player uses about half of that again (0.21-0.29 of a core on the same video).
- **The start-up screen no longer hangs.** While the logo showed, the app prepared five pages in advance, and the loading line froze 15-22 times (up to 1.4 s at once). That also made the start 2-3 s longer. The pages are now prepared after the start, while the app is idle. The loading screen is gone after about 1.5 s (was 5.4-5.9 s on a test copy of a real setup). The line only appears when the start takes a while and the window is calm, and fades in. Home is drawn for the first time under the still logo, not during the fade (the fade paused 2-5 times, now at most once for under 0.1 s).
- **AnimePahe posters show.** A Cloudflare pass only works with the identity of the browser that earned it. AnimePahe sent the pass it got from the app's browser with a phone browser's identity, so Cloudflare refused it: every poster failed (56 in one evening) and the extension reopened its check at each start. Pictures, and the extension's requests with the sandbox off, now carry the browser's pass and identity. Measured: posters 0 failures; no check window and no refusals at start (before: 1 refusal and the window).
- **Cloudflare sites work in the extension sandbox like outside it.** Cloudflare turns away the sandbox's own connection even with the right pass, so such requests were fetched one page at a time through the app's browser, and AnimePahe reopened its check at every start. Now the app makes a refused request for the sandbox, with its browser's pass. This only happens for sites whose check the browser passed, and never for addresses on your own network. AnimePahe with the sandbox on: 0 check windows, 0 browser fetches (was 26), search in 0.5 s, an episode starts in 1 s.
- **Arrow keys move around Home.** Left, Right, Up and Down go to the nearest card or button that way, like on a TV. The page scrolls to follow, and Up from the rows shows the art at the top whole again. Before, the arrows did nothing (only Tab moved).
- **Kisskh plays in under 2 seconds instead of hanging.** Its episodes are one big file that the player reads piece by piece. With the extension sandbox on, the app downloaded the whole 400 MB file for every piece: a 20-second stall, then the app could run out of memory. Now only the requested piece is fetched. First picture in 1.8 s; a jump to 25 minutes takes 1.6 s. The sandbox also passes video to the player in 1 MB pieces instead of 8 KB ones.
- **Live DASH channels start sooner too.** WILLOW FHD: 8.5 s before, 1.3-3.6 s now. JIO TV: 2.8 s. Like live HLS, the player gets the one quality that suits your screen. The quality menu lists every quality and reopens the channel on the one you pick (about 1 s). When two qualities have the same height, the menu also shows their bitrate.

- **Live channels start in about half the time.** A live HLS channel is now opened with one quality, the best for your screen (the player used to open and read every quality first). PTV SPORTS: 8.3 s to the picture before, 3.3-4.9 s now. The quality menu still lists every quality; picking another opens the channel on it (about 2 s). Recorded videos keep all their qualities as before.
- **Reload page in the site-check window**, for a check page that stays blank or keeps showing "Just a moment...".
- **The title bar comes back at the top edge in more cases.** The bar also appears when the pointer is at the very top of the screen the maximized window fills (it stayed hidden after making a new profile with a PIN on one PC).
- **Cloudflare cookies are the browser's.** Cloudflare cookies the app or an extension writes are no longer copied over the ones the check page is using.
- **Live channels start sooner.** The player reads the start of every quality of a live HLS channel before it plays; the app now fetches those pieces all at once (a 5-quality cricket channel: 8.3 s to the picture before, 5.9-7.7 s now).
- **Live DASH channels no longer fail at the first piece.** The player reads the first piece of a live DASH stream twice at the start (once to learn the stream, once to play); the app refused the second request (a rule against a repeat loop later in the stream), and some channels never started.
- **The source list marks the source you picked.** After choosing another source in the player, the list kept the first one highlighted as "Starting".
- **A wrong "the site no longer has this video" is gone.** The quick failure on HTTP 404 (previous change) also fired when the 404 came from the app's own live-stream helper; it now only applies when the address itself was not found.
- **A dead link fails at once, and says why.** A source the site answered "not found" for was opened a second way first, and the next source (or the message) came about 20 s later; now it is at once. The message says what the site answered: "the site no longer has this video (HTTP 404)", "the site refused it (HTTP 403)", "the site is limiting requests: try again in a minute", "the site has a problem right now" (it said only "loading failed").
- **Poster under the pointer:** the episode count gives way to the play and add buttons (half of "6 eps" showed beside the play button).
- **Extensions are ready about a second sooner after every start.** The sandbox program now starts from a start-up archive of its own classes (ready in 2.2 s instead of 3.2 s on a test PC) and no longer does the app's own chores at its start (Stremio add-ons, backups, a GitHub check: they also made network calls from the sandbox). The archive is made once per version by the app itself, outside the sandbox, a minute and a half after the start, and the sandbox may only read it.
- **Settings fixes.** *Preferred media* listed "Video" twice (the "Others" kind had the same name); the app language list showed "GB English", "FR Français" (Windows draws no flag emoji): it shows the names alone; *Download path* now says where downloads go before a folder is chosen.
- **No more camera (or microphone, location) requests.** Web pages the app opens in the background for extensions (a site check, a page with adverts in it) could ask for the camera, and Windows then asked you whether CloudStream may use it. Every such request is now refused silently, and the app's browser never touches a real camera or microphone.
- **The first video opens quicker.** The subtitle fonts were prepared on the first Play, on the thread that draws the window (about a third of that pause); they are prepared in the background at start now.
- **Calmer episode grid.** Episodes you have not reached yet no longer say "Description hidden to avoid spoilers" under every card, and the stills have no outline.
- **Smooth start of a video.** Pressing Play cut to a black window for about half a second, then the picture flashed in with a corner not yet covered, the loading screen came back over it, and the picture again. Now the loading screen (title, artwork, progress) shows as soon as the player page is drawn, the video stays hidden until the controls lie over it, and the loading screen fades out over the first picture.
- **A proper window for a site check of an extension in the sandbox.** When a source needs you to pass a Cloudflare check, the window now says which extension needs it and what to do, shows the page in a frame, the site it waits for with a working ring, and Cancel. It was a line of text, the page and a grey button.
- **The Cloudflare notice no longer gets in the way.** "Passing the Cloudflare check of ..." was a bar in the middle of the window after 5 s; it is now a small chip in the bottom right corner after 10 s (most checks pass before), with a stop button, and never over the player.
- **Settings drop-downs line up.** They were 110 to 240 points wide from row to row; they are one width now.
- **Extensions that need a browser keep working in the sandbox.** When the sandbox could not give an extension a browser page once (a Cloudflare check with too many pages open, or a PC without Edge WebView2), the whole extension was unloaded and never loaded again (AnimePahe, CineVood, Anichi, Comix in a test of 199 sources). Now only that one page fails and the extension stays in the sandbox. A test of 199 sources with and without the sandbox found these as the only ones worse in the sandbox; after the fix they do as well or better there (AnimePahe and CineVood Mirror play).
- **Stronger sandbox.** The extension program also runs with: DLLs from System32 first, every DLL and the heap at random addresses, end at the first heap corruption, Windows system fonts only, no crash dialog, and below-normal priority (a busy extension cannot make the app or the video stutter). All 25 extensions of a real setup load with these on.
- **Smooth edges in the player.** The thin outlines around the player's menus, buttons, badges and seek preview were drawn 1.5 pixels wide at 150 % scaling and looked jagged over a bright video; they are gone, the glass surfaces stand on their own.
- **Title page polish.** The description no longer runs under the *Up next* card; a title with one episode (an anime film, an OVA) has *Play* instead of *Play E1* and no *Up next E1* card; the source name stays readable over bright artwork; the tabs (Episodes, Related...) are a size smaller.
- **Smaller polish.** Search: an *All* chip (all kinds were eight white chips at once). Extensions: lower cards, round uninstall button like the settings one, no ring on the chosen repository. Downloads: no empty storage bar before the first download (the free space is in the subtitle) and a *Browse titles* button. Stats: tiles of equal height. Settings: descriptions written the same way (no full stop on one-sentence lines). Support prompt: one main button, the donation and Discord links side by side.

- **Hardware decoding setting, for green or flickering pictures.** Settings > Player > Video and the player menu: *Auto* (the graphics card, as before), *Compatible* (an older way of the graphics card, which fixes the green flashes some Intel and AMD drivers show) and *Off* (the processor decodes). A change applies to the playing video at once.
- **GoTaku (and other extensions with their own video server) plays again.** GoTaku serves its videos from a little server inside the extension; with the extension sandbox on, the app could not reach it ("That source did not work"). Those requests now go through the sandbox.
- **Torrent picker, "Select a torrent to stream".** Right-click an episode (or the title's *More* menu) > *Find torrents…*: searches Nyaa and AnimeTosho for the title, with smart search (only this episode, batches if you want), episode number, resolution, trusted-only and providers on the left, and the releases on the right with group, episode, resolution, dual audio / multi subs badges, seeders, size and age, sortable. A click plays it with the torrent engine.
- **Notifications look better.** Schedule > Notifications starts with an *Up next* card: the next episode of what you follow, with a live countdown, arrows to the others, and a line that fills as it gets closer. New-episode cards are bigger, show the episode that aired, how many you watched and how far behind you are, and their background comes forward under the pointer. Titles you are watching on a signed-in AniList account count as followed, and their progress is used, without any setup.
- **New episodes card at the start.** When new episodes are waiting, a small card in the bottom right corner says so ("EP 6 · Overgeared · +1 more"); a click opens the notifications.
- **Low-end PC mode starts quicker.** The loading screen no longer waits for the pages to be prepared or for smooth frames on a weak PC, which made the start look frozen.
- **Discord invite updated.** The old link in Settings > About and the support prompt had expired.
- **Shorter right-click menu on titles.** Play, Open title page, *Add to library ›* (Watching, Completed, On-Hold, Dropped, Plan to Watch open beside it; the list the title is on is ticked) and Favourites. Menus can have such side lists now.
- **No backward jump when turning the mouse wheel slowly.** A wheel often sends one stray notch the other way right after a real one; on slow scrolling that notch moved the page back each time. It is now ignored unless a second notch confirms the wheel really turned back. Touchpad scrolling also goes straight to the page. Settings > About > Copy diagnostics now lists the last wheel steps.
- **Smoother wheel scrolling.** Each notch now speeds up and slows down gently, and quick notches flow into one glide (each notch used to start with a jolt).
- **Reload on Home closes a check window.** An extension's "verify you are human" window or a Cloudflare check holding Home up is closed, then Home loads again.
- **Check windows at most once an hour.** An extension's check window is not put up again within an hour, also when the app is opened again. Updates are not affected.
- **Home reloads when its extension was just updated.** The update at start replaced the extension Home was showing: Home loads again with the new version.

## 1.1.0

- **PIN lock for any profile, the only one too.** A profile with a PIN asks for it when the app opens; until it is typed (on the keyboard) nothing of that profile shows, and the lock screen offers the other profiles or a new one.

- **Automatic subtitle timing (experimental).** Player menu > Subtitles > Delay and size: *Sync now* reads the next 2.5 minutes of the video's sound ahead in a hidden player (about 2 s on a fast connection) and moves the subtitle delay so the lines match the voices, then says so at the top of the picture ("Subtitles auto-synced · Moved by -2.0 s"). *Sync every video* (also Settings > Player > Subtitles > Auto subtitle sync, the same switch) does it once for each video. Works with subtitle files (not subtitles inside the video, not live streams), never touches the sound you hear, and changes nothing when it is not sure.
- **Audio delay**, and typed delays. Player menu > Audio delay moves the sound against the picture; a click on the delay number (audio or subtitles) lets you type it ("-350", "1.5 s").
- **Subtitle options first.** In the Subtitles menu, delay, style, encoding and the add/search actions come before the list of files.
- **Fast wheel scrolling no longer jumps backwards.** A wheel spun hard often sends one notch the wrong way; that notch used to cancel the scroll and pull the page up for a moment. The page also follows the wheel more closely.
- **Pages open smoothly right after the start.** The main pages are prepared while the loading screen is up (it stays about 2 s longer), so their first visit no longer stalls (first opens about 45 % smoother). The start-up archive also covers the title page, the player and every settings page now.
- **The app no longer gets stuck drawing slowly.** Closing it within 20 s a few times in a row counted as a graphics failure and moved it to a slower renderer for good. A start where you clicked or scrolled now counts as a good one.
- **Extensions no longer fail to load at random in the sandbox.** Two extensions loading at the same moment could break the shared list of providers (`ConcurrentModificationException`, "Could not load ...").
- **Shorter settings texts** (sandbox, updates, Stremio, mpv, screen translation), with all translations. The Telegram links are gone for now.
- **PIN lock for profiles, a screen dimmer, a sleep timer**, and dialog buttons that sometimes needed two clicks now take one.

## 1.0.9

**Apple TV look, extensions in a Windows sandbox, Ultima sync fixed, delete a profile and reset the app**
- **Ultima's sync works again with the extension sandbox on.** The sandbox ran Ultima in its own process, and that process only gets a copy of the settings without the library, history and accounts (kept out of it on purpose). Ultima synced that copy, and what it pulled from the cloud never reached the app. Ultima now runs in the app (the Java guard still watches it; the Extensions page says *Not in the sandbox: syncs the app's library*). Checked against a stand-in Firebase database on a copy of the data: sign-in registers the device and pushes resume watching, bookmarks, settings, search history and extensions; a change pushed from a simulated phone (a new title, a bookmark) shows up in *Continue watching* and in the Library without a restart; the guard refused nothing. The WipeGuard also stopped Ultima from deleting the repository list when it first met an empty cloud copy.
- **The Library follows changes made elsewhere.** It only reloaded on upstream's library event, so titles bookmarked through sync (or the Home page) showed after a click on Refresh. It now reloads when bookmarks or Home change. "1 titles" reads "1 title".
- **Plain words for errors.** A title page that failed showed the extension's raw exception ("Unrecognized token 'https': was expecting (JSON String ...) JsonParser.java 2675"). It now says what happened (the site could not be found, took too long, refused the request, no longer has the page, or the extension could not read the answer and needs an update), with the original text in small print below. Home does the same for a source that fails. A source that only needs setting up (Ultima before its sections are chosen) no longer says it "could not be reached" with a *Try again* that could never help: the notice says it has nothing to show yet and *Open its settings* opens the extension's settings page (`ProblemTextTest`).
- **Less work on the UI thread while title pages and the Explore page load.** TMDB answers (a title page's is about 100 KB) were parsed and written to the disk cache on the UI thread; a profile of the first seconds after start showed the file writes there. All of it runs in the background now.
- **A web address that is no host name no longer ends a network thread in `crash.log`.** The crash log of 26 October (`IllegalArgumentException: invalid host wildcard specification`) came from an extension that used an address pattern of its own (`https://*.m4uplay.*`) as an address: the Java permission check threw an exception that is no `IOException`, OkHttp passed it on to its dispatcher thread, and the app wrote a crash report for what is only a failed request. The DNS step now answers such a name like an unknown host (`UsableAddressesTest`). Found by reading the crash and hang logs of the real portable, then tried the other ways a session can go wrong: the extension sandbox killed in the middle of a session (the next request starts it again with its extensions, a search works), a Library of 500 titles (opens with a worst frame of 81 ms, scrolls at 31 ms), 90 seeks in a video (memory does not grow, 540 MB after leaving the player) and fifty fast page changes.
- **The tabs of a title page and of the Library are all in view in a window that is not maximised.** The row of tabs (*Episodes, Related, Recommended, More like this, Cast & crew, Gallery, Reviews, Details*; *Watching ... Subscribed* in the Library) kept the full size at any width, so in a window of about 1000 px the last ones were cut off (*Revie*) or hidden, and a mouse has no sideways wheel to reach them; on a title page the *52 Episodes* line sat beside the tabs and covered the last one too. The tabs now take the largest of three sizes at which every one fits (and only scroll, bringing the chosen tab into view, in a window narrower than the smallest size); the episode count moved to the line with the episode filters. Found by walking every page in windows of 1000, 700 and 640 px, with the frame statistics (every page opens at the refresh rate, the worst frame of a first open is 150 ms while search results stream in) and the text audit.
- **The look follows Apple TV.** Pages are pure black (or the page colour of the theme) with solid grey grouped surfaces (#1C1C1E, #2C2C2E under the pointer) and no outline round them, translucent grey controls and chips with nothing round them (the chosen one stays white), grey secondary text (60 % / 50 %), a large bold page title with tight tracking and a grey line under it (no coloured tile: the page tile is gone from the title of every page and of Settings, it stays where Settings lists a page), shelf titles with their arrow right after them (*Continue watching ›*, the line opens the list; the *View all* link is gone), a quiet grey icon for an empty page (no disc), white progress bars and numbers, second buttons over artwork (*+*, *i*, trailer) in translucent grey without a ring. Light mode follows the same idea (grey page, white grouped cards). Surfaces of the other colour themes (Slate, Nord ...) are unchanged. An accent glow behind the pages was tried and removed: it read as a faded haze.
- **AnimePahe's searches work through the sandbox.** Found in the log of the rebuilt copy: the first request after its check passed got HTTP 200 (its Home), the next ones (its search) 403 although the cookie was the one the browser had just earned, and the extension's own retry was throttled. Cloudflare ties a clearance to the handshake of the browser that earned it, which the sandbox's HTTP client does not have. A GET that a Cloudflare check refuses is now passed on to the app's browser (a hidden page, public addresses only, at most three at a time, only for a site whose check the rules allow: the extension is in use, the user did not cancel it, it did not fail in the last ten minutes) and the extension gets the text the page shows (a JSON document or the HTML), never the cookies. Checked in a copy of the real extensions: with AnimePahe in use, four different searches (naruto, one piece, bleach, attack on titan) gave 8 results each, about six seconds each; the same searches gave none while another extension was the one in use (nothing is opened for an extension that is not).
- **No gradient effects.** The fade of the page colour at the ends of rows that scroll sideways, the lit gradient edge of posters and cards, the gradient placeholder behind a poster, the sweep of light over a loading block, the fade under the top bar when a page has scrolled, and the fade of the skeleton of Home are gone: flat colours and one hairline. What stays is the dark blend over the banner's picture behind its text (without it the text cannot be read) and the shades of the player.
- **AnimePahe's pages open in a fraction of a second.** The browser fetch above loaded a hidden page for every request: AnimePahe asks for about ten addresses when its Home opens, so they queued behind each other (six seconds each), the *Passing the Cloudflare check* bar stayed up and the user pressed Cancel, which leaves the site alone for two minutes. The first request of a site now makes one hidden page (the others wait for it) and keeps it open for five idle minutes; every later request is one `fetch` inside that page (about 0.3 s). The real status of the site reaches the extension (a 404 or a 429 of the site is no longer hidden behind the check's 403). Checked: five searches in a row, each answered in about 0.3 s after the first page.
- **The Extensions page opens on the Installed list.** It opened on the first repository (the Mega repository, with one extension) whenever the list of repositories was already known, and showed that one extension until the page was left and opened again.
- **No Cloudflare window just for opening Search.** The empty Search page (Explore) counted as "a search in all extensions", so every extension was "in use" and the check window of one that happens to need a click (AnimePahe) came up over it. A window is now put up only for the extension chosen on Home, the one of an open title page, or the one a Home search is limited to; a search in all extensions may still pass checks in hidden pages but never opens a window, and the empty Search page does neither.
- **Poster and card details.** An extension is one compact row (icon, name and version, what it offers and where it runs, its buttons at the right) instead of a tall block with empty space; the progress line on posters and *Continue watching* cards is white; a poster rises a little more under the pointer.
- **Cards, in one place.** `cardSurface` is the solid card surface now shared by about twenty cards (Settings, Stats, Extensions, About, update cards ...); `glass()` uses it too.
- **"Passing the Cloudflare check of ..." no longer comes up every time a title is opened, and a hidden page is never heard.** Found in the log of playing Lupin from StreamPlay: opening it started the Cloudflare checks of five of its sites (cinevood, asiaflix ...), each held for its full minute and a half behind the bar, and one of the hidden pages played the sound of an advert video (the Edge page of the app, no picture, only sound) while mpv played the next link. Two faults and one rule: (1) the check of an extension in the sandbox never ended when the clearance came: the extension looks for it in the cookies while its page works, and the cookies reached the sandbox only when the page had ended, so every check ran to its timeout and the extension then went on without the clearance anyway (the sites of CloudflareKiller never worked in the sandbox). The app now watches the browser's cookies and ends the page when a new clearance comes, and the extension looks once more when the cookies arrive. (2) A check that nobody sees is waited for at most 25 s (it was 60 to 90), and a site that gave no clearance is left alone for 10 minutes, so the next title is not held up by it again. (3) The bar shows only after 5 s (it flashed at 1.5 s). All browser pages (Edge, and the bundled Chromium where Edge is missing) are started with `--mute-audio`: the app's video plays in its own player, a page of an extension has nothing to say.
- **Libraries**: Jackson 2.21.8 -> 2.22.4 and Ktor 3.6.0 -> 3.6.1; every other library of the catalog is at the release that Maven Central lists (Bouncy Castle 1.86, jsoup 1.23.2, Conscrypt 2.8.0, Coil 3.6.3, kotlinx 1.11/1.12, JNA 5.19.1, Gson 2.15.0). OkHttp stays on 4.12, the version the extensions are built against (5.x changes what they call).
- **Clean-up**: the unused helpers that were left in the Fluent controls and the player (hand cursor, scrim brush, right-of-anchor popup, two offset helpers), an unused image-type pattern and view accessor in the bundled-Chromium engine, an unused path-direction helper, an unused timestamp in the window chrome and an unused update accessor are gone; a scan of every top-level name of the desktop package finds nothing unreferenced any more.
- **Extensions page**: an installed extension's card says what it offers and where it runs (*Runs in the Windows sandbox*, or why it does not) and how many sources it has; the cards of extensions without a description were empty in the middle.
- **Extensions run in a Windows sandbox.** Extensions are third-party programs; they now run in a separate process that Windows itself locks down (an AppContainer in a job object), so whatever an extension does it cannot read your documents or browser data, change files outside its own folder, start a program, reach your home network or this PC's loopback addresses. It keeps the internet, so sources work as before: search, title pages and links come back over a pipe, a Cloudflare check is done by the app's browser for it, and the cookies come back. **Local lists showed the United States**

**The setup program updates an MSI install in place**
- Many people installed from the MSI. Running the `-setup.exe` over such a copy used to put a **second** copy beside it (another folder, another entry in Apps, a desktop shortcut of the same name that the MSI's removal later deleted). The setup now asks Windows Installer for the MSI product (the upgrade code is the same in every MSI since 0.1.0, per-user or per-machine), says so on its last page, **closes the running app** (a close request first, so it saves and ends the normal way; ended only after 20 s), removes the MSI product with `msiexec /x` (no window; the Windows administrator prompt only for an all-users product) and installs into **the same folder**, so pins and shortcuts stay valid. Settings, accounts, extensions and history are in `%APPDATA%\CloudStream` and are never touched; a `data` folder next to the exe (the 0.1.3 / 0.1.4 layout, which the MSI's removal would delete) is moved there first. A copy that is already newer than the setup is left alone with a message; a registration whose folder is gone is cleaned away. From then on the app is a Setup install and its updater downloads the `-setup.exe`.
- Not changed: MSI copies keep updating through the `.msi` of a release (the updater picks the file that fits how the copy was installed), so a release should carry the `.msi`, the `-setup.exe` and the portable zip.
- Tested on a throwaway MSI product (own upgrade code, own folders, the real installer script with its test knobs): MSI copy with the app running and a `data` folder; MSI copy not running; no MSI at all; an MSI newer than the setup (setup stops, nothing changed); an MSI registration whose folder was deleted by hand. **"Trojan:HTML/Redirector" in the cache folder (Windows Security report)**
- Windows Security named a file in `%APPDATA%\CloudStream\cache\http_cache\` (`<hash>.1.tmp`). It was a copy of a **web page** that an extension's streaming site had sent (an advertising / redirect script): the app keeps answers of the sites it asks in a cache so that repeated requests are quick, and it kept pages too. The page was never run by the app and the file was removed by Windows Security; nothing is installed or changed. Web pages (HTML, scripts, SVG, event streams) are **no longer written to the disk cache** at all, data (JSON, images ...) still is, and what earlier versions had put into the folder is removed once at the next start. Tested with a local server: a page is asked for every time and is not found in the folder, the same JSON answer comes from the cache.

**The installer no longer starts PowerShell**
- The MSI ran three PowerShell commands (`-ExecutionPolicy Bypass -WindowStyle Hidden`) while installing: a console window flashed and the command line looks exactly like what malware does, so people were right to find it shady. They only moved the `data` folder of 0.1.3 / 0.1.4 installs aside during an upgrade, which the installers since 0.1.5 never create. They are gone: no custom action of the MSI runs a script or a shell any more (`tools/patch-msi.ps1` checks that when the MSI is built). The setup program (`-setup.exe`) never had any. The updater runs its small script with no window at all.

**White area at the start**
- A window that was saved maximized (with a smaller normal size) could open with its picture drawn at the old size in the top left and the rest of the screen white. The check that lays the window out again looked every half second and needed a third of the window to be missing for 2 s; it now looks every 150 ms for the first 20 s (0.6 s of difference), notices a missing 4 % of the window, and, when laying out and re-sending the frame messages do not help, makes a maximized window normal and maximized again (the old last step did nothing for a maximized window). Every step is written to `logs/app.log` ("the picture is ... but the window is ..."). I could not make it happen on this PC in 5 launches (also with all 16 processor threads busy); the check is built on the screenshot of the report.

**Video that hangs on a weak PC**
- The standard player draws every frame with the processor. A fast PC needs 4 ms for that; an old or very small one cannot keep 15 frames a second. Every 5 s the player now looks at the dropped frames of mpv and at its own rendering time: two slow windows in a row lower the cost by one step, **smooth motion off** and frames dropped in the decoder, then a **lighter picture** (about 1.1 million pixels, enlarged by the graphics card, and the decoder's fast paths), then fewer (0.56 million, loop filter skipped on frames nothing refers to). A short message in the bubble says so; the steps stay for the rest of the run, also for the next videos. If it is still slow after the last step a window offers the **GPU player** (Settings > Look > Native GPU player), which lets the graphics card do the work. Nothing changes on a PC that keeps up.
- Not measured on a weak PC (none here): the thresholds are the dropped frames (10 % of the film's frames) or a rendering time above 70 % of a frame. Dev: `/player?perf=0..3` sets the step, the line has the last note.

**The app updates itself again, properly**
- **Update now** in the update window downloads the new version with a progress bar (it can be hidden; Settings > About shows it too), checks the file against its size and the SHA-256 GitHub states for it (only files of this repository's releases are downloaded), and then asks for a restart. *Restart and update* closes the app the normal way (settings are written first), installs the update with no console window and opens the app again. Nothing is installed without that click, and never in the middle of a video: while a video page is open the dialog waits and Settings > About has the button.
- It uses the installer that fits how the copy was installed: the **setup program** (`-setup.exe`, silent with its progress window), the **MSI**, or, for a **portable folder**, the **portable zip** unpacked over the program folders (`app`, `runtime`) with `data` left alone. A development run or a release without the matching file opens the release page instead.
- The check runs a few seconds after the start and then every few hours while the app stays open (never during a video); a message says "CloudStream was updated to ..." the first time a new version starts. The update log is `update.log` in the `cloudstream-update` folder of the temp folder.
- A release should carry all three files (`CloudStream-<v>-windows-x64.msi`, `...-setup.exe`, `...-portable.zip`); the page of the release stays the fallback.

**Home banner**: rotates through up to 8 titles (it showed 3), all loaded in one go and shown once: it used to come up with three and grow a moment later, which made the strip of slices jump. The titles load in parallel; the rows of Home no longer wait for them (the banner is a skeleton of its own size until it is there, normally 3 to 5 s), a slow title is left out after 5 s once three are there (15 s at most), and the artwork TMDB has for the first two slides is fetched with them so the first picture is complete.

**Explore: Global and Local**: films and series have a *Global / Local · <country>* switch (the country is the one in Settings > Appearance > Title information). Local lists titles made in that country: trending, in cinemas, most watched, top rated, new, and with a *Language* row (for India: Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Kannada, Bengali, Marathi, Punjabi, English); genre and streaming-service filters apply to both. The choice is remembered.

**Pause screen**: after a pause that lasts (controls away) the picture dims with a vignette and shows the title's logo (the extension's, TMDB's when it has none, the name otherwise), what is paused, one line (year, age rating, genres), the story (the episode's, else the title's), the time, and a thin progress line at the bottom edge. The parts come in one after the other; playing again takes it away at once.

**Title bar in the player**: the title bar that comes down at the top edge of a maximized or full-screen video (it is drawn by the controls window over the video) could not be used to move the window. Pressing its empty part and dragging moves the window (a maximized one comes back to its normal size under the pointer, dragged to the top edge of the screen it maximizes again), a double click maximizes or restores.

**A blank window at the start on a busy PC**: the video window's object was created by a warm-up thread 2.5 s after the start; when the first composition of the window was still running then (a slow start, little free memory) it read state made after its snapshot, the window logged "Reading a state that was created after the snapshot was taken" and stayed an empty grey rectangle while the app kept running behind it (found with 1.1 GB free). Its state, and the updater's, are now created on the UI thread before the first composition.

## 0.1.8 (pre-release)

Live TV playlists that did not play now do, AnimePahe's home page and Cloudflare window work, and no more frozen window while Cloudflare is being solved.

**Updating from 0.1.7:** the app tells you about this version a few seconds after it starts. Choose *Open download page*, download the installer and run it over the old one: your settings, accounts and extensions are kept (this upgrade was checked on a real install).

**Extensions**
- **Extensions that declare classes inside a function work again.** The converter that turns an extension (dex) into something this app can load dropped the "declared inside this function" mark of such classes, so Kotlin reflection (used by the JSON reading of many extensions) failed with "Unresolved class". The home page of **AnimePahe** (Phisher repository) was blank because of it; other extensions may have been affected in the same way. Every installed extension is converted once more on the first start after the update.

**Cloudflare**
- **The window no longer freezes while Cloudflare is being solved.** The dialog that extensions such as AnimePahe open for the "Just a moment" check asks the browser for its cookies every second on the screen's own thread, and while the browser was busy with the challenge page each ask held the whole window (up to 0.7 s each, so it seemed stuck). After a slow answer the app now does not wait for a few seconds.

**Player**
- Live DASH channels whose address sends the player on to another server now play. Many playlist channels (for example the DRMlive playlist in the M3U Playlist Player of CNCVerse) answer with a redirect to a CDN that adds a token; the app asked that CDN's host for the segments with the *first* host's path, got 404 for every one and gave up with "unrecognized file format". It now takes the segments from where the manifest really came from, passes the token of the redirect on with them, and keeps the cookies the CDN hands out (as a browser does). The "Activate Playlist" entry of such playlists is a real 10 to 13 second clip behind the same kind of redirect, so it could never play and the playlist stayed inactive; it plays now.
- HLS channels whose playlist address redirects to another server (for example the Sony channels of the tsiptv playlist) play now: the addresses inside the playlist were resolved against the first server and answered 403.
- Live channels whose segments are numbered in hex (`$Number%08x$`, used by Sling-based channels such as Willow) play now: ffmpeg only knows decimal numbers, so the numbers are translated by the app, and the CDNs named by the manifest's absolute BaseURLs are reached through it.

**Known**
- A Cloudflare check that needs you to click "Verify you are human" still needs that click. AnimePahe's own window shows the check inside the app so you can click it (it clears by itself in about 10 seconds most of the time). Extensions that use the app's built-in Cloudflare handler (no window) can still fail on such sites after waiting about a minute; a window for those is planned.
- Some playlist channels still do not play: entries the source itself has taken down or blocks (403/404, expired links), Widevine-protected channels (no decryption module on a PC), and playlist entries whose keys the CNC "M3U Playlist Player" extension does not pass on (for example the `jtv.tsiptv.xyz` entries and the Jio TV+ entries of premiumplugx).
